# apollonian (heavy source notes)

Files: `library/sources/apollonian/apollonian.fs` (identical copy in `src/main/resources/default_sources/apollonian/`). Desktop only, GLSL 3.30 core, `LSD_COST` 3, `is3D`. Built after `pseudo_kleinian` and shares its structure (helpers, camera, colour, loop pattern).

## What it is
A fly-through of an Apollonian-style sphere gasket. Space is tiled into cubic cells; each iteration wraps the point into its cell (translation), optionally turns it about y (Twist), and inverts it in a sphere centred in the cell when it lies inside. The surface is the preimage of a core sphere; spheres tangent to the cell spheres appear in the gaps at every level. The camera flies along the cell-corner channel (x = y = cell half-width), which stays open for the default values.

## Loop caps and cost
- Constant caps `MAX_ITER` 12, `MAX_STEPS` 120, `MAX_AO` 4; loops break at the quality-driven counts. Default (Quality 0.08): 6 iterations, 54 steps, 1 AO tap. Max: 12, 120, 4. Min: 6, 48, 1.
- Worst case per pixel: steps x iterations map() evaluations (about 324 at default, 1440 at max), plus 6 for the normal and up to 4 for AO at the hit. No textures or float buffers.
- Measured (headless GL, NVIDIA RTX A2000 Laptop GPU via PRIME offload, 1920x1080, glFinish-timed 5-draw average, default look, GL_RENDERER confirmed NVIDIA): Quality 0.08 (default) 8.7 ms (about 115 fps); Quality 0: 7.8 ms; Quality 0.15: 10.7 ms; Quality 0.25: 15.5 ms (the earlier default); Quality 1: 27.3 ms (about 37 fps). The lower default has fewer fine inversion levels but keeps the sphere-gasket look. Not a desktop A2000, not sustained; indicative only.

## Mathematical derivation
Only from the mathematics; no existing shader code consulted.
- Four mutually tangent spheres generate an Apollonian gasket by repeated inversion in the spheres tangent to them (Apollonius of Perga; Soddy; Lagarias, Mallows and Wilks, "Beyond the Descartes circle theorem", 2002, for the group-theoretic picture). The 3D case with spheres of radius equal to the half-spacing of a cubic lattice (tangent neighbours) is the group generated by lattice translations and one inversion.
- Cell wrap `mod(p + b, 2b) - b` is the translation part: an isometry. Rotation about y is an isometry. Inversion in a sphere of radius R is p -> R^2 p / |p|^2, which is p * k with k = R^2 / |p|^2 and has local length scale k; the code uses k = clamp(R^2/|p|^2, 1, kmax), the identity outside the sphere.
- Distance estimate: a map scaling lengths locally by s sends an image distance d to about d/s in the source (Lipschitz argument). dr is the product of the k factors, so d = (|p_N| - c) / dr. It is approximate where kmax clips; the march step is 0.8 d.
- Orbit traps (Hart): closest approach to the origin, closest approach to the inversion sphere, inversion count.
- Hit threshold is half a pixel footprint (0.5 * t * fov / height), also the basis of the normal epsilon.

## Known issues
- Seen only as headless renders on the dev machine (viewed as PNG), not in the running app or under modulation.
- Extreme settings give flat or empty frames (never NaN): small InversionRadius (spheres no longer tangent, the structure collapses to flat walls), CoreSize 1 with InversionRadius high (camera inside solid), CoreSize 0 with InversionRadius 1 (sparse spheres on a flat field). The default region is rich.
- Visible faint seams along the cell-centre axes in some views (the wrap is exact but the normal epsilon straddles it).
- With Twist non-zero the structure loses the strict lattice symmetry and the open camera channel may close.
- Default was lowered from 0.25 to 0.08 (owner request) for two-deck headroom; the detail of the smallest spheres is reduced versus Quality 0.25.
